## 2026-10-15 run 1

Did: #19 is still blocked, and "CI compiles every report to a PDF" still waits on someone seeing CI. That left one slot, and #82's note left an open question: two different runs could drive one live bench at once. I took that up. I filed "One process drives each instrument" (serves Safe anywhere) and closed it in one step. `Bench.role` now takes a host-wide non-blocking `flock` (`msvcrt.locking` on Windows) on each live instrument's address: its VISA resource or serial port, else its driver and device or tool. The lock files live in `npie-instruments/` under the temp dir, or in `NPIE_LOCK_DIR`. The bench holds them until `Bench.close()`, which `Run.release` now calls. A driver that fails to open lets go of its lock. Two roles on one address share their bench's hold. A refused run aborts without writing to the other run's supply, its own end-of-run OUTP OFF included, so its `safe_state` says "supply not confirmed off". New `tests/test_npie_instrument_lock.py` has 8 tests, one of them a real second process that is SIGKILLed while it holds a lock. New `tests/conftest.py` gives every test its own lock dir. design.md section 3 and npie_run's docstring say so. `make check`: 290 passed, 9 skipped, 79 s. With instruments.py and runner.py reverted, 4 of the 8 new tests fail. In one of them, run B set its own voltage on A's supply and went on to the next human step. I also added #83's Closed line and pointed the run-lock goal at #83.
Decided: Lock per instrument address rather than one host-wide bench lock. That needs no answer from the owner on whether the laptop is only ever one bench, and two benches that share no instrument still run side by side. A refused run aborts rather than waits, which matches `run.lock`. Its safe_off is refused too, so it never turns off a supply another run has on. Lock files stay in place after release, for the same race reason as `run.lock`.
Learned: The existing live tests never close their benches, and a `pytest.raises` traceback keeps a Bench alive. With a shared `/tmp` lock dir, tests would refuse each other, and so would two checkouts running `make check` on this host at once, hence the conftest. A `Bench` built as a temporary is freed at once by refcounting, and that closes its lock file. My first child-process test fell into exactly that.

Its `resume` then carries on from a supply state it didn't leave. A resume that checks the bench is as the run left it (supply setpoint and output read back) would close that.
